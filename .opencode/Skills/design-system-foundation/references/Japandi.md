# Japandi

## Overview

Japandi is the convergence of Japanese minimalism and Scandinavian warmth—a design philosophy that values calm, craftsmanship, natural materials, and intentional living. Rather than pursuing minimalism as an aesthetic, Japandi treats reduction as a way to elevate quality, allowing every object, texture, and space to have purpose.

The style combines the Japanese philosophy of **wabi-sabi**, which embraces imperfection and impermanence, with the Scandinavian concept of **hygge**, emphasizing comfort, warmth, and everyday wellbeing. Swiss-inspired grid discipline and modern functionalism provide an additional structural foundation, resulting in interfaces that feel quiet, balanced, and timeless.

The visual experience should never feel empty. It should feel peaceful.

---

# Design Philosophy

Less is only meaningful when what remains has purpose.

Silence is part of the composition.

Materials should speak louder than decoration.

Imperfection creates warmth.

Empty space is an active design element.

Design should slow the user down instead of demanding attention.

The experience should communicate:

- Calm
- Quality
- Simplicity
- Craftsmanship
- Warmth
- Balance
- Authenticity
- Longevity

Nothing exists without intention.

# Visual Language

Japandi creates emotional warmth through restraint.

Rather than adding visual interest with decoration, it relies on:

- texture
- proportion
- rhythm
- spacing
- natural color variation
- material authenticity

Interfaces should feel handcrafted instead of manufactured.

The eye should move slowly across the layout.

---

# Typography

Typography should feel quiet and architectural.

Words should never overpower the interface.

Instead, typography supports the overall feeling of calm.

### Characteristics

- Neutral sans-serif families
- Japanese-inspired proportions
- Open tracking
- Comfortable line-height
- Medium or regular weights
- Large margins
- Minimal hierarchy jumps

Typography should feel like an object placed carefully inside a room.

---

# Color Direction

Color comes from nature rather than screens.

The palette is intentionally restrained.

### Primary Palette

- Warm Sand
- Soft White
- Charcoal
- Natural Wood

### Supporting Palette

- Sage Green
- Stone Grey
- Dusty Rose
- Deep Indigo

Colors should appear:

- muted
- earthy
- warm
- tactile
- timeless

Pure black and pure white should generally be avoided.

Temperature matters more than saturation.

# Layout Principles

Whitespace is structural.

Every gap serves a purpose.

### Layout Characteristics

- Asymmetric balance
- Large margins
- Open composition
- Quiet rhythm
- Intentional negative space
- Moderate content density
- Horizontal breathing room
- Calm visual flow

# Component Direction

## Navigation

Simple horizontal navigation with generous spacing.

Minimal visual weight.

Thin separators when needed.

---

## Hero

Large breathing room.

Minimal copy.

Strong photography or natural imagery.

One focal message.

---

## Cards

Flat surfaces.

Subtle shadows.

Soft corners.

Minimal borders.

Large internal padding.

Cards should resemble carefully crafted objects.

---

## Buttons

Quiet but confident.

Muted colors.

Comfortable padding.

Minimal elevation.

Interactions should feel gentle rather than energetic.

---

## Forms

Simple fields.

Thin borders.

Comfortable spacing.

Large touch targets.

Minimal validation styling.

Clarity over decoration.

---

## Empty States

Use subtle illustrations, ceramics, branches, paper textures, or minimal line drawings.

Never overfill empty states.

---

## Decorative Elements

Decoration comes from:

- texture
- grain
- material
- spacing
- composition
- subtle organic shapes

Avoid ornamental graphics.

---

# Motion

Motion should almost disappear.

Animation exists only to support calm.

### Characteristics

- Slow fade-ins
- Gentle upward movement
- Soft opacity transitions
- Long easing curves
- Minimal hover elevation
- Smooth scrolling
- Natural pacing

Movement should feel like breathing.

Never energetic.

# Avoid

Japandi is generally unsuitable for:

- Gaming
- Sports brands
- Music festivals
- Children's products
- Entertainment platforms
- High-energy campaigns
- Flash sales
- Meme culture
- Neon aesthetics
- Loud social media campaigns

Whenever excitement is the primary objective, Japandi loses its emotional strength.

# Modern UI Translation

Modern Japandi applies timeless craftsmanship to contemporary digital products.

Retain:

- Natural materials
- Warm neutrals
- Spacious layouts
- Quiet typography
- Organic asymmetry
- Functional simplicity

Modernize:

- Responsive systems
- Accessibility
- Performance
- Component libraries
- Design tokens
- Micro-interactions

Avoid:

- Sterile minimalism
- Empty white screens
- Cold grayscale palettes
- Excessive gradients
- Glassmorphism
- Decorative excess
- Visual noise
- Sharp industrial geometry

The objective is not minimalism.

It is calm human-centered craftsmanship.
